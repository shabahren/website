Methods

Methods grounded in research practice.

I use interpretive and structured qualitative methods to study political language, causal beliefs, strategic narratives, and foreign-policy decision-making. My approach has developed through thesis research, policy publications, institutional projects, multilingual source analysis, and formal training.

Core methods

01

Qualitative Content Analysis and Document Analysis

I design and analyze corpora of speeches, policy documents, white papers, institutional publications, interviews, and public records. The process includes explicit source-selection criteria, contextual coding, comparison across texts, and interpretation of recurring concepts and causal claims.

Applied in: the M.A. thesis; research on China’s narrative of international order; work on Chinese economic diplomacy; the Iran–China strategic-narratives project

02

Cognitive Mapping and Causal-Belief Analysis

Cognitive mapping provides a structured way to reconstruct causal beliefs expressed by political actors. I identify causal statements, standardize concepts, code positive and negative relations, and analyze how beliefs about problems, instruments, goals, and consequences form a connected system. I use the maps as interpretive evidence rather than as a substitute for historical and institutional analysis.

Applied in: Xi Jinping’s Perceptions and the Belt and Road Initiative; current comparative research on Iranian and Chinese strategic narratives

03

Interview and Thematic Analysis

My institutional and commissioned work has included interview transcription, organization of practitioner and elite accounts, thematic coding, and synthesis of interview material with documentary evidence.

Applied in: China’s Rise: Dimensions and Implications for Iran; the commissioned report on Chinese negotiating culture

04

Multilingual Source Analysis

My academic training in Chinese and professional experience in political translation support research across Persian, English, and Chinese sources. I work with Chinese-language material at a strong research-reading level and use linguistic context to reduce reliance on decontextualized translations of political concepts.

Applied in: China-focused publications, policy research, political translation, and corpus development

A transparent, researcher-led workflow

  1. 01Define the research problem and unit of analysis.
  2. 02Build and document the corpus.
  3. 03Code language and causal relations.
  4. 04Reconstruct and compare structures.
  5. 05Interpret findings in political and institutional context.
  6. 06Validate decisions and report limitations.

Methods training

In 2024, I completed the Spring and Summer Research Methodology Boot Camps organized by the Iranian Political Science Association. The programs covered scientific reasoning, applied theory, hypothesis formulation, qualitative research design, hermeneutics, discourse analysis, grounded theory, NVivo, data-driven research, academic writing, and creative problem-solving. This training broadened my methodological literacy; the core methods presented above are those I have subsequently used or developed in my own research.

Developing computational capacity

I am developing capabilities in R, statistics, Python-assisted corpus processing, and computational text analysis. I am particularly interested in using language models to support corpus preparation and extraction of candidate causal relations from larger collections of political texts. These tools extend, rather than replace, researcher-led interpretation: source traceability, manual validation, and transparent coding decisions remain central.
