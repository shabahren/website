About

Mohsen Mofidi Chelan

From language and translation
to foreign-policy research.

I am an International Relations researcher specializing in Chinese foreign policy, China–Middle East relations, and strategic narratives. My research examines how leaders and institutions interpret international change, how China explains its identity and role in world politics, and how Chinese narratives are received, adapted, and used by other actors.

My academic path began with a B.A. in Chinese Language at Allameh Tabataba’i University. Studying Chinese gave me direct access to the language and political concepts through which China describes its development, foreign relations, and place in international order. I later completed an M.A. in International Relations at the University of Tehran, where my thesis used cognitive mapping to investigate Xi Jinping’s perceptions and the Belt and Road Initiative.

My interest in political language also developed through years of translating politically consequential interviews and speeches during successive crises in U.S.–Iran relations. Working in a news environment where small differences in wording could affect interpretation made me attentive to the relationship between language, identity, political judgment, and foreign-policy debate.

Iran’s growing emphasis on relations with China, public debate surrounding long-term Iran–China cooperation, and my research experience at the Center for Chinese Research brought these interests together. Subsequent work at the Tehran International Studies & Research Institute and the Center for Scientific Research and Middle East Strategic Studies allowed me to develop them through policy publications and institutional research.

Alongside my academic work, I manage a multimedia translation department at Tabnak News Website. The role involves multilingual production, team coordination, and decision-making under demanding deadlines. It has also encouraged me to design workflows and use digital tools and automation to improve consistency, accuracy, and speed. This practical experience informs my developing interest in computational research, while my academic work remains grounded in transparent, researcher-led qualitative analysis.

I am currently developing research on the reception of Chinese strategic narratives in the Middle East and on the relationship between rhetorical adoption, changes in causal beliefs, and foreign-policy interaction.

Short biography for conferences and publications

Mohsen Mofidi Chelan is an International Relations researcher specializing in Chinese foreign policy, China–Middle East relations, and strategic narratives. He holds an M.A. in International Relations from the University of Tehran and a B.A. in Chinese Language from Allameh Tabataba’i University. His research examines leadership perceptions, the construction and reception of Chinese narratives, and their implications for foreign-policy interaction. He uses qualitative content analysis, cognitive mapping, strategic narrative analysis, and comparative qualitative methods. He is currently a Visiting Researcher at the Center for Scientific Research and Middle East Strategic Studies in Tehran.
