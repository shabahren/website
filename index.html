Chinese Foreign Policy · China–Middle East Relations

How do perceptions and narratives shape China’s foreign policy?

I am an International Relations researcher studying Chinese foreign policy and China–Middle East relations. My work examines how leaders interpret international change, how China constructs narratives about its role in world politics, and how those narratives are received, adapted, and used by other actors.

Research agenda

From foreign-policy interpretation to narrative reception.

My research connects two levels of foreign-policy analysis. At the level of decision-making, I examine how leaders’ perceptions and causal beliefs shape the definition of problems, priorities, and policy instruments. At the level of international interaction, I study how China presents its identity and preferred international order, and how political actors in the Middle East interpret, adapt, resist, or strategically use those narratives.

Across both levels, my central concern is how political actors understand their environment, communicate that understanding, and translate it into foreign-policy choices.
